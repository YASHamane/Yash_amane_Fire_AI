# Income-tax rules for a salaried resident individual: FY 2024-25 (AY 2025-26)

Scope: salary income only, resident individual. All amounts in whole rupees.
Source tags like [S1] refer to `rules_data/sources.md` (official Income Tax Department pages).
This text is the ONLY law input given to the LLM implementations (b) and (c).
Wording is a paraphrase of the official material, not a copy.

## 1. Order of computation (author's summary of how the pieces fit together)

For EACH regime, in this order:
1. Start from gross salary.
2. Subtract the HRA exemption (old regime only, section 7).
3. Subtract the standard deduction (section 3). Result: income from salary.
4. Subtract home-loan interest on a self-occupied house (old regime only, section 6). Result: gross total income. It cannot go below zero.
5. Subtract the Chapter VI-A deductions 80C, 80CCD(1B), 80D (old regime only, section 6), each limited to its cap. Their sum cannot exceed gross total income. Result: total income.
6. Round total income to the nearest Rs 10 (section 2).
7. Compute tax on total income using the slabs (sections 4 and 5).
8. Subtract the section 87A rebate (section 8). Result: tax after rebate.
9. Add surcharge, applying marginal relief (section 9).
10. Add 4% cess on (tax after rebate plus surcharge) (section 10).
11. Round the final amount payable to the nearest Rs 10 (section 2).

The regime with the lower final amount payable is cheaper. If equal, say so.

## 2. Rounding [S9]

- Section 288A: total income is rounded to the nearest multiple of Rs 10 before tax is computed.
- Section 288B: the amount payable is rounded to the nearest multiple of Rs 10.
- In both cases paise are ignored first (dropped, not rounded). Then a last rupee digit of 5 to 9 rounds up, and 0 to 4 rounds down.
- Only total income and the final payable amount are rounded. Intermediate steps are not.

## 3. Standard deduction on salary, section 16(ia) [S2, S3]

- Old regime: Rs 50,000, or the salary if that is lower.
- New regime (section 115BAC(1A)(ii)): Rs 75,000, or the salary if that is lower.

## 4. Old regime slabs, rates for AY 2025-26 [S1]

Below 60 years:
- up to 2,50,000: nil
- 2,50,001 to 5,00,000: 5%
- 5,00,001 to 10,00,000: 20%
- above 10,00,000: 30%

60 to 79 years:
- up to 3,00,000: nil
- 3,00,001 to 5,00,000: 5%
- 5,00,001 to 10,00,000: 20%
- above 10,00,000: 30%

80 years or more:
- up to 5,00,000: nil
- 5,00,001 to 10,00,000: 20%
- above 10,00,000: 30%

Each rate applies only to the part of income inside that band.

## 5. New regime slabs, section 115BAC(1A) [S1, S4]

Same for every age:
- up to 3,00,000: nil
- 3,00,001 to 7,00,000: 5%
- 7,00,001 to 10,00,000: 10%
- 10,00,001 to 12,00,000: 15%
- 12,00,001 to 15,00,000: 20%
- above 15,00,000: 30%

## 6. Deductions

OLD regime only [S1]:
- Section 80C: up to Rs 1,50,000.
- Section 80CCD(1B): up to Rs 50,000, in addition to 80C.
- Section 80D health insurance premium, self, spouse and children: up to Rs 25,000, or Rs 50,000 if the person is a senior citizen (60 or more).
- Section 80D, parents: up to Rs 25,000, or Rs 50,000 if a parent is a senior citizen. This limit is separate from the self limit.
- Section 24(b), interest on a home loan for a self-occupied house (loan taken on or after 1-4-1999 for purchase or construction): up to Rs 2,00,000.

NEW regime: none of the above is allowed. HRA is also not allowed. Only the standard deduction applies (section 3). [S1]

## 7. House Rent Allowance exemption, section 10(13A) and Rule 2A (old regime only) [S8]

The exempt amount is the LEAST of:
1. the HRA actually received in the year;
2. rent paid in the year minus 10% of salary;
3. 50% of salary if the house is in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata or Chennai, otherwise 40% of salary.

"Salary" here means basic salary plus dearness allowance. If no rent is paid, the exemption is zero. If rent minus 10% of salary is negative, the exemption is zero. HRA received minus the exempt amount stays taxable (it is part of gross salary already).

## 8. Rebate under section 87A (resident individuals) [S7]

Computed on the tax from the slabs, before surcharge and cess. Based on total income AFTER rounding.

Old regime: if total income is Rs 5,00,000 or less, rebate = the lesser of the tax and Rs 12,500. No rebate above Rs 5,00,000. No marginal relief. No age condition.

New regime (section 115BAC(1A)):
- If total income is Rs 7,00,000 or less: rebate = the lesser of the tax and Rs 25,000.
- If total income is above Rs 7,00,000 and the tax is more than (total income minus 7,00,000): rebate = tax minus (total income minus 7,00,000). So the tax payable becomes exactly the excess over 7,00,000 (marginal relief).
- Otherwise no rebate.

## 9. Surcharge and marginal relief [S1]

Surcharge is a percentage of the tax after rebate. It applies to the WHOLE tax when total income exceeds the threshold.

Old regime: 10% above 50,00,000; 15% above 1,00,00,000; 25% above 2,00,00,000; 37% above 5,00,00,000.
New regime: 10% above 50,00,000; 15% above 1,00,00,000; 25% above 2,00,00,000. There is no 37% tier.

Marginal relief: if total income exceeds a threshold T, then tax plus surcharge must not exceed (tax plus surcharge payable on income exactly T) plus (total income minus T). If it does, the surcharge is reduced until it does not. [S1 states the rule for thresholds 50 lakh, 1 crore, 2 crore, and 5 crore for the old regime.]

Interpretation note (to verify on the official calculator): "tax plus surcharge payable on income exactly T" includes the surcharge rate that applies at income exactly T, which is the previous tier's rate (nil at 50 lakh).

## 10. Health and education cess [S1]

4% of (tax after rebate plus surcharge), in both regimes.
